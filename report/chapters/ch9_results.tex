\section{Reinforcement Learning Analysis}

\subsection{Q-Table Convergence}

The Q-Learning agent begins with a fully exploratory policy ($\epsilon = 0.2$) and converges toward preferring the ``Accelerate'' action (0.5$\times$ multiplier) in low-congestion states and the ``Maintain'' action (1.0$\times$) in medium states. In high-congestion and critical states, the agent learns to throttle traffic to prevent cascading queue failures.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Converged Q-Table Policy (Representative)}
\label{tab:qtable}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Action} & \textbf{Low} & \textbf{Medium} & \textbf{High} & \textbf{Critical} \\
\midrule
Accelerate (0.5$\times$) & \textbf{Best} & Good & Poor & Poor \\
Maintain (1.0$\times$) & Good & \textbf{Best} & Good & Poor \\
Throttle (2.0$\times$) & Poor & Poor & \textbf{Best} & \textbf{Best} \\

\section{Digital Twin Health Metrics}

The Digital Twin composite health score provides a holistic view of node viability. Starting from $H = 100\%$, the score degrades as battery depletes and anomaly density increases. Key observations:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Under normal operation (no DDoS), health degrades gradually following the battery curve.
    \item During DDoS injection phases, the anomaly density spike causes a rapid health drop, even when battery remains adequate.
    \item The exponential moving average ($\alpha = 0.05$) smooths transient anomaly spikes while preserving trend information.
\end{itemize}
